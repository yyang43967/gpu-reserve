# Contact and escalation

## Lab administrators

The lab administrators manage the servers, labs, accounts, quotas, and approvals. Reach them at the lab support address listed on the Help page, or in person during office hours. For anything about a specific reservation, include its ID or the server and time.

## Requesting a quota change

If the default weekly quota is not enough for your work, email the lab administrators. Explain the project, how many GPU-hours per week you need, for how long, and why the work cannot be spread over more weeks. A note from your advisor helps. The administrators decide each request and set the new quota on your account; the change applies to new bookings immediately.

Increases are usually granted for a fixed period, such as the weeks before a deadline. The administrators set your quota back by hand when the period ends; the system does not do it automatically.

## Hardware faults during a booking

If a GPU or server misbehaves during your booking, such as ECC errors, a GPU missing from `nvidia-smi`, crashes, or overheating, stop your job and report it to the lab administrators with the server name, GPU numbers, the time, and the error messages.

### Urgent problems

If the fault stops your work, end your booking early so the unused hours return to your quota, then report it. Mark the message as urgent in the subject. The administrators will switch the server to maintenance if needed and tell you whether it can be fixed the same day. Repairs that need replacement parts can take several days, so book another server for the rest of your work.

## Disputes about approvals

If you think a rejection or a cancellation was wrong, first reply to the email you received with more detail about your job. If you still disagree, ask your advisor to contact the lab administrators. Disagreements that cannot be settled that way go to the faculty member responsible for the lab's computing resources, whose decision is final.

## Response times

The administrators answer messages within one working day. Urgent hardware problems during working hours are usually looked at within two hours. There is no support at night or on weekends; plan long runs with checkpoints so they can survive a fault outside working hours.
